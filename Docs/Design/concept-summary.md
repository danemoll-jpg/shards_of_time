# SHARDS OF TIME

## 3D Platformer Game Concept Summary

Genre: 3D Action-Adventure Platformer / Collectathon\
Style: Colorful, stylized 3D cartoon adventure\
Players: Single-player, with character switching\
Inspiration: Banjo-Kazooie, Super Mario Odyssey, Spyro the Dragon\
Structure: Eight interconnected worlds with exploration, combat, puzzles, collectibles, and unlockable abilities.

### 1. Story and premise

Based on the original Shards of Time film, the game follows Dan, Abi, and their cat Kira after Kira accidentally breaks a mysterious Christmas ornament, scattering its fragments across time.

The trio must travel through eight different historical and futuristic worlds, collecting time shards and finding their way home.

The game expands upon the film with additional locations, enemies, puzzles, challenges, and gameplay sequences while preserving the original story's major events, characters, humor, and ending.

The mysterious violinist continues appearing throughout the adventure.

### 2. The eight worlds

| World                  | Setting and gameplay                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Ice Age             | Glaciers, frozen caves, mammoths, ice bridges, prehistoric creatures                   |
| 2. Ancient Egypt       | Pyramids, temples, traps, ancient machinery, hidden chambers                           |
| 3. Bethlehem           | Ancient villages, rooftops, markets, stealth, environmental puzzles                    |
| 4. Medieval Kingdom    | Castles, knights, tournaments, towers, and the introduction of Muscle Man              |
| 5. Pirate Seas         | Pirate ships, tropical islands, shipwrecks, and extensive underwater exploration       |
| 6. Victorian Steampunk | Clock towers, mechanical contraptions, steam machinery, industrial environments        |
| 7. Tomorrowland        | A retro-1950s vision of the future, flying vehicles, robots, futuristic technology     |
| 8. The Moon            | Low-gravity platforming, lunar environments, alien hazards, and the story's conclusion |

Each world features its own enemies, environmental hazards, puzzles, collectibles, and unique gameplay mechanics.

### 3. Playable characters and abilities

Players can switch between Dan, Abi, and Kira. Each has different strengths.


Dan: Strength and general platforming

Basic attack: Punch.

Can push objects, climb, and perform general physical tasks.

Possible upgrades include charged punches, ground pounds, and improved climbing.


Abi: Speed and agility

Basic attack: Kick.

Specializes in precise jumping, fast movement, and reaching difficult platforms.

Possible upgrades include double jumping, wall jumping, spinning kicks, and aerial dashes.


Kira: Transformations and exploration

Basic attack: Claws.

Can squeeze through small passages, find hidden areas, and perform specialized tasks.

Transforms into something different in every world, gaining unique abilities.


Muscle Man: Extraordinary strength

Joins the party during the Medieval Kingdom chapter.

Acts primarily as an AI companion who can move enormous boulders, lift heavy machinery, break barriers, and help access previously unreachable areas.

### 4. Kira's transformations

Similar to the transformations in Banjo-Kazooie, Kira takes a different form in each world. Each transformation provides a unique ability needed for certain challenges.

The actual transformations from the film should be preserved where possible, with additional abilities or transformations introduced for gameplay.

| World               | Proposed transformation | Special ability                                    |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Ice Age             | Prehistoric cat         | Powerful jumping, clawing through ice              |
| Ancient Egypt       | Royal Egyptian cat      | Activating ancient mechanisms                      |
| Bethlehem           | Sheep                   | Blending into flocks, bouncing                     |
| Medieval Kingdom    | Small dragon            | Gliding, breathing fire                            |
| Pirate Seas         | Otter (film canon)      | Fast swimming, deep diving, underwater exploration |
| Victorian Steampunk | Clockwork cat           | Operating mechanical systems                       |
| Tomorrowland        | Robot cat               | Hacking computers, controlling machines            |
| The Moon            | Space Kira              | Low-gravity movement and thruster jumps            |

These are working gameplay proposals, except for the confirmed otter transformation.

### 5. Time shards and portal progression

Time shards are the game's primary collectibles.

Unlike the film, where the story progresses directly from one era to the next, the game allows players to travel backward and forward between worlds once the appropriate portals have been activated.

Shard collection rules:

- Shards are generic, interchangeable collectibles, not unique fragments assigned to specific ornaments.
- Each world contains numerous shards hidden throughout its environment.
- Players must collect enough shards to activate a portal and continue the adventure.
- Collecting every shard in a world is not required to progress.
- Shards contribute to a shared collection total and are not consumed when unlocking portals.
- Once a world's portal is activated, players can return to that world.
- Some shards are deliberately inaccessible until players learn new abilities or acquire new companions.

For example, players might encounter an enormous boulder in the Ice Age that blocks several shards. They cannot move it initially.

After meeting Muscle Man in the Medieval Kingdom, they can return to the Ice Age and have him move the boulder, revealing the hidden shards.

The game therefore rewards revisiting earlier locations with new abilities.

### 6. Unlockable moves and exploration

As the game progresses, characters learn new moves that expand their capabilities.

Abilities serve multiple purposes, including combat, platforming, puzzle-solving, and reaching new areas.

Examples include Abi learning a double jump, Dan acquiring a ground pound, or Kira gaining advanced abilities associated with specific transformations.

New moves should remain useful across multiple worlds rather than existing solely to open one particular door.

The main storyline should always remain completable without requiring players to obtain every collectible. Optional areas and secrets provide additional challenges for completionists.

### 7. Combat and enemies

Combat is light, accessible, and integrated into platforming rather than being the game's main focus.

Each character has a basic attack:

- Dan punches.
- Abi kicks.
- Kira uses her claws.
- Muscle Man handles heavy physical obstacles and can assist in certain encounters.

Characters can unlock additional combat moves as the game progresses.

Each world features enemies appropriate to its setting.

| World               | Possible enemies                               |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Ice Age             | Sabertooth cats, wolves, prehistoric creatures |
| Ancient Egypt       | Mummies, scarabs, temple guardians             |
| Bethlehem           | Hostile guards, dangerous animals              |
| Medieval Kingdom    | Knights, archers, armored enemies              |
| Pirate Seas         | Pirates, crabs, dangerous sea creatures        |
| Victorian Steampunk | Clockwork spiders, mechanical guards           |
| Tomorrowland        | Robots, security drones                        |
| The Moon            | Alien creatures, lunar defense machines        |

Selected worlds may feature mini-bosses or major encounters involving character abilities, environmental puzzles, and platforming.

Not every world requires a conventional boss.

### 8. Health and upgrades

Players begin with three hearts representing their health.

Taking damage removes hearts, while healing pickups restore them.

Permanent health upgrades are hidden throughout the game and can increase the maximum health up to a proposed limit of ten hearts.

All playable characters share the same health pool.

Additional upgrades may improve combat abilities, movement, or specialized character skills.

Health upgrades provide another reason to explore previously visited worlds.

### 9. Pirate Seas: Special story sequence

The pirate chapter preserves an important event from the original film.

During the adventure aboard a pirate ship, Muscle Man discovers a time shard and becomes tremendously excited.

Unfortunately, his enthusiasm leads him to accidentally drop the shard overboard.

The shard falls into the sea, forcing the group to dive after it.

This becomes a major underwater gameplay sequence, featuring submerged caves, reefs, shipwrecks, currents, underwater enemies, and hidden treasures.

Kira's transformation into an otter becomes particularly important here. Her swimming abilities allow her to reach areas inaccessible to the others.

The underwater environment also offers additional collectibles and secrets that players can return to find later.

### 10. Collectibles and completion

The game features several kinds of rewards.

| Collectible      | Purpose                                                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Time Shards      | Primary collectible used to unlock portals and progress |
| Health Upgrades  | Permanently increase maximum health                     |
| Ability Unlocks  | Give characters new moves and gameplay capabilities     |
| Memory Fragments | Optional story moments and additional background        |
| Hidden Secrets   | Bonus challenges, cosmetics, and rewards                |

Each world includes a completion tracker displaying the number of shards found, optional upgrades discovered, and other secrets remaining.

Players can complete the main storyline without finding everything.

Full completion is an optional goal for those who want to explore every corner of the game.

### 11. Story structure and ending

The primary story follows the film's sequence through all eight worlds.

However, activated portals allow free travel between previously unlocked eras.

This gives players flexibility to explore, search for missing shards, and use newly acquired abilities in earlier locations.

The final Moon chapter brings the narrative to its conclusion.

The shards are reunited, time begins to rewind, and the ornament reforms.

Dan and Abi awaken on the couch, unsure whether their extraordinary adventure truly happened.

The mysterious violinist appears outside, Kira notices the ornament, and the story ends as it did in the film.

After the credits, an optional free-roam mode allows players to revisit worlds and finish collecting everything without changing the original ending.

### 12. Development approach

The game should be built around reusable systems rather than individually scripting every interaction.

Key systems include character switching, third-person movement, combat, health, collectibles, portal travel, unlockable abilities, companion interactions, and persistent world completion.

The recommended first milestone is a playable Ice Age level demonstrating core movement, combat, a Kira transformation, shard collection, portal unlocking, and an area that can only be reached after learning a future ability.

Once the foundational systems are reliable, the remaining worlds can build upon them with unique environments, enemies, transformations, and challenges.

## Overall vision

Shards of Time should feel like a classic, colorful 3D platforming adventure with modern controls and cinematic storytelling.

Players explore eight distinct worlds, fight enemies, solve puzzles, unlock abilities, collect time shards, and discover secrets. As their characters grow more capable, previously inaccessible areas become reachable, encouraging exploration and replayability.

The game retains the heart and humor of the original film, particularly the relationship between Dan, Abi, Kira, and the wonderfully accident-prone Muscle Man.

The guiding philosophy: Easy to learn, fun to explore, rewarding to master, and full of surprises.

This gives us a solid foundation for the eventual full game design document and development plan.
